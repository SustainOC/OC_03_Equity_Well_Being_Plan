How each indicator is measured

EW-K1 and EW-K2 count a group only when a program, initiative or network names it. STARS excludes commitments, plans, statements, recruitment and programs open to everyone, which is why the College's employee policies do not count on their own.

EW-K3 and EW-K4 follow the STARS definition: the assessment covers students and employees and lets the experience of underrepresented groups be compared with that of dominant groups. The headline tile reports the same work as a share of full-time equivalent people covered.

EW-K5 to EW-K11 come from one disaggregated data set. People who have not self-identified are counted in the dominant group, and binary records may be reported where that is all that exists, so the figures can be produced before self-identification is complete. Any figure about a group is reported at the level the College already publishes.

EW-K8 divides the average annual net price of attendance for low-income students by the local minimum wage for students. EW-K11 compares each group's completion rate with the overall rate; 0.90 or more is equitable in STARS.

EW-K12 to EW-K15 are inventories of services and published terms. Services run with the students' unions count where the College actively takes part. Leave counts where government and College benefits together pay 60 per cent of regular pay or more and the job is protected.

EW-K16 needs assessments covering at least half of employees within three years. EW-K17 uses a family-of-four living wage for the main campus location, Kelowna, and counts remuneration including qualifying benefits. Accreditation as a living wage employer earns the full 3 points on its own.

EW-K19 measures the desired outcome directly. It is paired with the sustainability and climate check Policy / Planning is adding to the policy framework, so one review covers both. EW-K20 tracks ownership, which is where this area starts.

Where this stands today

Okanagan College already runs much of what this area is scored on: dedicated support for students with disabilities and for Indigenous students, counselling and health coverage, a smoke-free policy across every campus, published rules on harassment and safe disclosure, and two plans for inclusion and accessibility approved by executive leadership. What it does not yet have is the measurement STARS asks for: a recent climate assessment, representation and completion figures by group, and a pay equity and living wage position.

No Okanagan College figure on this page is estimated. Positions marked evidenced rest on College documents and have not yet been scored by AASHE. Peer figures are labelled as theirs.

Where these numbers come from

The 25 points come from the STARS 3.0.1 technical manual: Representation & Access is 14 (3, 3, 2, 3 and 3) and Wellbeing & Work is 11 (3, 3 and 5). Pay Equity and Living Wage drops to 4 if the College has no significant contractors.

The smoke-free point is worked out from the College's Smoke-Free Policy, approved 13 August 2025 and effective 1 September 2025. Smoking is prohibited in every occupied building (0.5) and restricted outdoors to designated areas (0.5). A fully smoke-free campus is worth the same single point, so it adds nothing in STARS.

The 11 points are Racial and Ethnic Representation (3), Gender Parity (2), Affordability and Access (3) and Student Success (3).

The peer figures are from the public AASHE scorecards of the University of the Fraser Valley (STARS 3.0 Gold, 69.12, April 2026) and the University of Northern British Columbia (STARS 3.0 Gold, 68.83, March 2026), read 26 October 2026. They are their figures.

The situation

The College has done the groundwork on equity and inclusion. Its inclusive community plan for 2025 to 2030, approved in summer 2025, sets four pillars with dated actions for 2025/26 and 2026/27, including an evaluation plan built with the College's data teams. An inclusive college community policy took effect on 31 July 2026. The accessibility plan for 2023 to 2026 was shaped by about 1,400 participants, and every campus now has an outdoor accessibility audit.

STARS asks for something narrower and more exact. It scores support that names each underrepresented group, a climate assessment of students and employees within three years with its results published, representation, affordability and completion figures by group, and a pay equity and living wage position. Commitments and policies, however good, do not count on their own.

That is where the gap sits. The College's last climate surveys date from 2022, outside the three-year window. No group-level figures for representation, affordability or completion are in hand yet, and the living wage position is not known. The Wellbeing & Work credits have owners; the five Representation & Access credits do not.

So this plan does not write a second equity plan. It names owners, hands the data teams the STARS definitions early, cites the inclusion and accessibility plans' own actions, and asks for the measurement and disclosure that turn existing work into scored evidence.

What the peers have done

The two STARS 3.0 Gold institutions in the province score 13.00 and 10.95 of 25 on these credits (their figures). Both do well on wellbeing and employee rights.

Both score zero on student success by group, and close to zero on racial and ethnic representation. No peer in the sweep publishes completion rates by group: room for the College to lead.

The Equity & Well Being case

How measuring and publishing what the College already does turns into points, and then into better decisions.

The College does not need new equity programs to score here. It needs to count and publish what its own plans already do, at the level of detail STARS asks for.

Programs exist

Support services, the inclusion plan and the accessibility plan are already in place.

Evidence documented

Each program is matched to the group or criterion STARS names.

Groups measured

Representation, completion and pay are counted by group and published in aggregate.

Gaps acted on

Each gap the data shows goes into the inclusion plan's next cycle.

Two approved plans already govern this subject.

The inclusive community plan and the accessibility plan each carry their own actions, owners and reporting cycles. This plan asks for measurement and disclosure, and cites their actions rather than writing competing ones.

Eleven points ride on one data capability.

Representation, gender, affordability and completion all need figures by group. The inclusion plan's evaluation work is building that capability now. Handing its data teams the STARS definitions in Year 1 costs little and decides whether the 2028 report can carry these credits at all.

STARS scores what is named, measured and published.

Support counts only where a program names the group. The climate assessment earns half its point only once results are public, and the pay equity assessment earns 0.4 of its point only on publication.

The rest of the picture

Self-identification gaps lower the score; they do not block the report.

People who have not self-identified are counted in the dominant group, and binary records may be used. The College can report in 2028 and improve the figures after.

A fully smoke-free campus earns nothing more.

Designated outdoor areas already earn the full point. Any proposal to remove them stands on health grounds alone.

The living wage test uses one figure.

STARS takes a family-of-four living wage for the main campus location, Kelowna, not a figure per campus. Accreditation as a living wage employer earns the full 3 points without the percentage test.

Wellbeing & Work is the stronger half, at the College as at its peers.

Health, counselling, safety committees and published employee protections are likely in place. The work there is documentation.

The manual's own arithmetic does not always add up.

Five tables in this area sum to 1.002, 1.001, 1.002, 0.99 and 0.99 against stated maxima of 1. They join the inquiry to AASHE already open from other modules.

The carbon linkNot a carbon area. No activity here moves emissions, and no tonnes are counted. The one real link is access: transit is the main way to campus for many people who cannot drive, and Transportation carries that work and its accessibility fixes.

The detail behind each option

Axis adopted: how far the College measures and discloses what happens to its own people. It follows from the current state's own finding that this plan should ask for measurement, disclosure and STARS evidence rather than programs, and it separates three options that differ in what the College knows and shows.

Rejected: dependence on outside parties. Student support, people and culture and the data teams carry every option equally.

Rejected: dependence on outside money. Only the living wage involves cash, so the axis separates two options, not three.

Rejected: commitment beyond what is required. Representation is scored on outcomes, so a commitment ladder rewards statements STARS does not count.

Rejected: campus coverage. Every credit here is institution-wide.

The decisions we need

Who should own the five Representation & Access credits?

Recommended: equity and inclusion leadership as owner, with institutional records as the data lead. The Wellbeing & Work credits already have owners; these five do not, and every step after Year 1 waits on this.

Should the November 2026 report claim smoke-free, employee rights and admissions?

Recommended: yes, once each is confirmed. The smoke-free point is already worked out; the employee protections and the admissions position need a documentation check, not a program.

Which option should the College take?

Recommended: option 2, measure and publish by group. It makes every credit reportable by 2028 at no capital cost and gives the inclusion plan the evidence it needs to target its next cycle.

Should the College decide on living wage accreditation now?

Recommended: no, decide in FY2029/30 once the living wage share is measured and the cost is known. Accreditation is worth the full 3 points, but it commits a recurring cost.

Should this area supply the equity objective for the Sustainability Plan?

Recommended: yes. Policy / Planning needs one measurable racial equity and social justice objective with a lead; the inclusion plan already holds several. The point is counted there, once.

Should this plan cite the inclusion and accessibility plans rather than add programs of its own?

Recommended: yes. Both are approved by executive leadership with their own owners and cycles. This plan asks for measurement and disclosure, and adds a program only where STARS names a group no current program serves.

Settled, not reopened: Representation & Access is assigned to this impact area (23 October 2026). Wellbeing & Work is assigned here through its credit owners (26 October 2026).
